## Design Context

### Users
Smart Doc Flow is a professional document-intelligence workspace for human operators working with AI, not a casual consumer chat product.

Primary users are people who need to analyze, extract, verify, organize, and operationalize information from complex documents with AI assistance. In practice this includes document analysts, research and knowledge workers, internal data-processing operators, and technical users who need to inspect parsing results, extraction evidence, structured outputs, and downstream knowledge artifacts.

The product should also assume a secondary "AI-readable" requirement: interfaces, outputs, and workflows should stay structured enough that AI-assisted actions can continue across steps without losing context. The UI is therefore designed for human decision-making with strong machine-assist continuity, not for purely manual office work and not for autonomous black-box AI operation.

### Brand Personality
The product should feel:

- precise
- trustworthy
- intelligently capable

The emotional target is calm confidence rather than spectacle. Users should feel that the system helps them see deeply into documents, keep context under control, and move from raw unstructured material to verified structured knowledge.

The tone should be professional, evidence-aware, and technically fluent. It should not feel playful, over-marketed, overly futuristic, or conversational for its own sake. Even when the assistant is friendly, the overall experience should still communicate rigor, grounding, and operational usefulness.

### Aesthetic Direction
The current codebase and design specs point to a document-centric technical workbench, especially for Module 2 and Module 4.

The preferred direction is:

- professional analysis workbench, not a generic admin dashboard
- document-first and evidence-first, not chat-first
- blue-slate structural base with restrained accent colors
- high information density with clear hierarchy
- strong panel logic, stage-based layouts, and operator efficiency
- refined motion and feedback, but never decorative overload

The interface should look suitable for serious document analysis, extraction review, trace verification, structured querying, and knowledge operations. It should avoid styles that read as trendy AI demo UI, neon sci-fi dashboard, consumer productivity toy, or glass-heavy template output.

The visual benchmark is not one specific brand clone, but a blend of:

- a disciplined technical workbench
- a premium document-analysis console
- a trustworthy AI-assisted operations surface

### Design Principles
1. Put document truth before decorative UI.
Every major interaction should help users inspect source material, extracted structure, evidence, or downstream actions with less ambiguity.

2. Optimize for human-AI collaboration, not pure chat.
The system should feel like an intelligent workspace where AI assists analysis and execution inside a larger document workflow.

3. Preserve evidence, traceability, and context.
Outputs, corrections, structured facts, and assistant actions should feel grounded and reviewable, especially in extraction-heavy flows.

4. Use professional visual hierarchy over novelty.
Favor clear staging, panel boundaries, readable density, and deliberate emphasis instead of flashy styling patterns or generic AI chrome.

5. Make complex workflows feel controlled.
Parsing, extraction, verification, schema design, querying, and graph-related operations should feel connected and manageable, even when the underlying pipeline is sophisticated.

6. Design for depth without clutter.
The interface can be information-rich, but it should remain legible, layered, and purposeful. Density is acceptable; confusion is not.
